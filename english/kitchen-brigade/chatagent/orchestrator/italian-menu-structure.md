# Classic Italian Menu Structure

## Traditional Course Sequence

| Course | Role in the Meal | Responsible Chef |
|---|---|---|
| Starter (*Antipasto*) | Opens the meal and whets the appetite, typically in smaller portions | Starters Chef |
| First Course (*Primo*) | Dish based on pasta, rice/risotto, soup, or gnocchi | First Courses Chef |
| Main Course (*Secondo*) | Primary protein course (meat, fish, or plant-based centerpiece) | Main Courses Chef |
| Side Dish (*Contorno*) | Accompanies the main course with vegetables or light preparations | Side Dishes Chef |
| Dessert (*Dolce*) | Closes the meal on a sweet note | Pastry Chef |
| (Wine) | Accompanies the entire dining progression | Sommelier (optional) |

Note: in most real-world dining settings, the side dish is served alongside the main course rather than as a separate course; when presenting the final menu, you may group "Main Course + Side Dish" together if it reads more naturally.

## Formality Tiers and What They Entail

- **Casual / convivial**: generous portions, simple and comforting preparations, fewer courses (sometimes starter + first course + dessert, skipping the main course).
- **Elegant / special dinner**: 4–5 well-balanced courses, more refined techniques, and attentive plating.
- **Formal / business or ceremony**: full multi-course menu, measured portions, premium ingredients, refined plating, and predictable service pacing.
- **Banquet / large event**: dishes engineered to hold up reliably when produced at scale and held at service temperature.

## General Portion Guidelines (per Guest)

- Starter: 80–120 g
- First Course: 80–100 g dry pasta/rice, or 250–300 ml for soups/veloutés
- Main Course: 150–200 g protein
- Side Dish: 100–150 g
- Dessert: 100–150 g or one individual portion

These figures are indicative benchmarks rather than rigid rules: the responsible station chef may fine-tune them based on the dish.

## Adapting to Dietary Restrictions

When the user specifies dietary constraints (vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, or specific allergies/intolerances), those constraints apply across **every** course — not just the most "obvious" one. For instance, a gluten intolerance affects fresh pasta in first courses, breading or flour-thickened sauces in main courses, and pastries or sponge bases in desserts.
