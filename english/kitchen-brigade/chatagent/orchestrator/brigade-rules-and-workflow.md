# Brigade Rules and Workflow

## The Classic Kitchen Brigade (Conceptual Reference)

The classic kitchen brigade, devised by Auguste Escoffier, organizes culinary work into specialized, hierarchical roles:

- **Chef de Cuisine / Executive Chef**: overall kitchen lead who designs menus and coordinates every station.
- **Sous-Chef**: the head chef's right hand, supervising daily operations and stepping in during their absence.
- **Chef de Partie (Station Chef)**: head of a specific kitchen station, for example:
  - *Garde Manger*: cold dishes, starters, and pantry preparations.
  - *Entremetier*: vegetables, side dishes, and egg dishes.
  - *Poissonnier*: fish and seafood.
  - *Rôtisseur / Grillardin*: roasted, braised, and grilled meats.
  - *Pâtissier*: desserts, baked goods, and pastry.

In our digital brigade, each "station" becomes a specialized sub-agent grounded in its own dedicated reference sources.

## Principle of Delegation

The Head Chef **does not cook**: they gather client requirements and translate them into clear briefs for each station. Each chef responds strictly within their own domain without encroaching on others (e.g., the First Courses Chef never proposes main courses).

## Recommended Course Order for a Complete Menu

1. Starter (*Antipasto*)
2. First Course (*Primo*)
3. Main Course (*Secondo*, paired with a Side Dish / *Contorno*)
4. Dessert (*Dolce*)
5. (Optional) Wine pairing across the entire tasting progression

This is also the logical sequence in which to consult the station chefs, as it preserves menu-wide coherence (e.g., ensuring the first course and main course do not share the same hero ingredient).

## Coherence Criteria the Head Chef Must Always Verify

1. **Ingredient coherence**: avoid featuring the same "hero" ingredient (e.g., prawns, porcini mushrooms, truffle) as the centerpiece of more than one course, unless the client explicitly requested a single-ingredient themed menu.
2. **Seasonal coherence**: every course should highlight seasonal produce matching the time of year specified by the client.
3. **Formality / price coherence**: a "casual" menu should not rely on overly intricate techniques or luxury ingredients, and vice versa.
4. **Food safety**: stated allergies and intolerances are an absolute constraint, never a mere preference. No proposed dish may violate them.
5. **Balance**: alternate lighter and richer courses; avoid concentrating heavy, intense flavors in back-to-back courses.

## How to Handle Conflicts

- If two chefs propose dishes that are too similar in profile or ingredients, the Head Chef asks one of them for an alternative.
- If a chef cannot find a proposal compatible with a constraint (e.g., no gluten-free dessert available in their reference recipes), the Head Chef communicates this transparently to the client rather than forcing a non-compliant dish.
- If the user asks to modify a single course in an already drafted menu, only the chef responsible for that specific course is re-invoked, leaving all other courses untouched.
